Work Concepts II

Three ways to show all 15 projects so you can tell what each one is at a glance. The pictures are the real covers and figures from the site. The four projects without one yet (ONR, the swim intake, the playground and the pool alarm) get a drawing.

Concept DThe workbench

Your bench from above, on a green cutting mat. Every project is an object on it: the robot, the LED panel playing, the scope showing a hop pattern, two radios talking, the test tank, a folder for the sponsored work, a run sheet, a clipboard, a sketch of the pool alarm. The four client sites are prints next to the laptop; point at one and it comes up on the screen. hover or tap anything on the bench

Swipe sideways to see the whole bench. On the real site, phones would get the bench as a scrollable shelf.

Why it fits

Everything reads as what it is, even before you tap. It's the most personal: it looks like the place the work actually happens.

Watch out

It's the most drawing to build and keep current. Each new project needs a new object on the bench.

Concept A, revisedThread map with pictures

The same transit map, but every station is a picture of the project. The lines still tell how your work connects: the signals line runs into the pool alarm, where it meets the family line. Point at a station for a bigger look; tap a line to follow it. hover a station

Stations are in the order each project started. Spacing is not to scale.

Why it fits

Now you see the work and the story at once. Still a quiet, readable map, not a gimmick.

Watch out

Small round pictures crop screenshots hard. The hover preview and the card below carry the detail.

Concept FThe carousel

Living tiles on a merry-go-round. Each project rides on a pole and bobs as it turns. The ones facing you play a small live version of the project: the LED panel dances, the radios' symbols settle, rays bend through the water, the scope hops, the sites pan across their own screenshots. The one in front shows its details below. drag to spin, tap a tile, or use the arrow keys

Why it fits

It's playful, it moves, and every tile shows what the project does instead of naming it. The live tiles reuse demos the site already has.

Watch out

You only see a few projects at a time, so it needs a plain list under it for scanning and for screen readers.

How I'd use them

These can work together. The carousel or the workbench makes a strong top of the Work page: someone lands and immediately sees a robot, an LED panel, radios and websites. The thread map fits lower down, as "how it all connects," for people who want the story. Any of them links each project to its case study.
