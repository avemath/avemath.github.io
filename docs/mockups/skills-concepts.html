Toolbox Concepts

Three ways to show the eleven skills, each a nod to communications. All three keep the same facts: Know well or Have built with, the projects that use each skill, and the file it lives in. Try them, then pick one (or mix).

Concept AThe spiral flip book

Your idea. A ring-bound book of skill cards, flipped over the coil like a desk calendar. The tabs on the side are the index, so the whole list is always visible. Each card carries its own little waveform. click the card, drag it up, or use the arrow keys

Concept BTune in

An old radio dial. Each skill is a station; the taller the marker, the more projects use it. Drag the needle and the scope goes from static to a clean signal as you lock on, then the skill comes in. drag the dial, or use the arrow keys

Concept CA skills constellation

Data art. The skills are points on a constellation diagram: the ones I know well on the inner ring, the ones I've built with on the outer ring, like 16-APSK. Projects sit around the edge. Hover or tap a skill and its links light up to every project that uses it; hover a project to see every skill it took. hover or tap a point

Know well, inner ringHave built with, outer ringProjects, around the edge

How they compare

All three work with mouse, touch and keyboard, and fall back to a plain list for reduced motion.

A. Flip book

The most tactile and the most "you": paper, rings, tabs. Shows one skill at a time with a full card. Easiest to read on phones.

B. Tune in

The strongest communications story: finding a station in the static. Playful and memorable. One skill at a time, but the dial shows them all.

C. Constellation

The most data-art: it shows how everything connects at once, which recruiters can read in seconds. Densest of the three on phones.
