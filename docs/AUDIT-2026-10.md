# Portfolio audit, October 2026

Scope: the live design after the `redesign-2` merge (commit `1ded9fb`). Pages reviewed: home, /work/, /about/, /services/, /work/v2v-bsm-ofdm/ and /work/grace-mae-alterations/, at 1440px in the light theme and 390px in the dark theme, scrolled through first so reveals fired. Copy was read on the same pages and in `src/data/`. Research only: no site code changed.

## Verdict

The site is distinctive, honest and technically strong: the spectrogram, the How it works tab, the sound channel and the in-browser demos prove engineering skill in a way few student portfolios can, and accessibility and performance are excellent. Its biggest weakness is length and repetition, especially on phones, where the home page runs about 19 screens and the strongest evidence for each audience is spread thin. The fixes below are mostly about editing and ordering, not new features.

## Top 8 improvements, ranked by impact

### 1. The home page is 19 screens long on a phone
- **Problem:** at 390px the home page is 16,022px, about 19 phone screens. Featured work (4,465px) and Client work (3,827px) alone are 52% of it. A recruiter on a phone has to scroll through six full tiles and four full site rows to reach research or contact.
- **Evidence:** home, Featured work and Client work at 390px. Each featured tile is about 650 to 700px tall (art, chips, summary, outcome line, case study link). Each client row repeats the frame, summary, "What I did" and three metrics.
- **Fix:** a compact phone layout only, below 720px. Featured tiles become a row: small art, title, type, one line, the case study link; the summary, outcome and chips stay on desktop and in the case study. Client rows show the frame, title, kind line and metrics, with "What I did" folded into a native disclosure. Target: under 10 screens.
- **Effort:** M
- **Helps:** everyone, recruiters most (they read on phones between other tasks).

### 2. No client voice anywhere
- **Problem:** the only quotes on the site are from two professors. There is no line from any site owner, so a website client has to take my word for the experience of working with me.
- **Evidence:** `src/data/testimonials.json` has two approved quotes, both academic. /services/ (process, Live and in use, FAQ) and the home Client work section have none.
- **Fix:** ask each owner for one or two sentences about working together, with their permission (the content guide already requires the owner's OK). Show them in the Client work rows and on /services/ next to Live and in use. The testimonial component and approval flag already exist.
- **Effort:** S in code; depends on the owners.
- **Helps:** website clients.

### 3. Research labs get the least to go on
- **Problem:** the research section is careful and correct, but a lab or faculty reader leaves without a thesis direction, a list of methods, or anything they can cite or follow up on. The strongest public evidence of research method (BELLHOP notebooks, the signals playground, the V2V receiver) sits under Featured work or /work/, not under Research.
- **Evidence:** home, Research section: the public description, sponsor, partner, advisor and role, and two related links (Integer internship, V2V). BELLHOP, which is the most research-like public work, is not linked from the Research section.
- **Fix:** within the public wording rule: add BELLHOP and the signals playground to Related work; add a one-line "Research interests" in my own words (underwater acoustic communication, signal processing), if I am happy to state it; link the advisor's public LSU page. Add a thesis title or abstract only when it is public.
- **Effort:** S to M
- **Helps:** research labs.

### 4. The bottom of the home page says the same thing twice
- **Problem:** the Contact section lists email, LinkedIn, GitHub and Baton Rouge with a form. The footer directly under it opens with "Have something in mind? Email is the fastest way to reach me." and then lists email, GitHub, LinkedIn and the résumé again in the packet frame.
- **Evidence:** home, Contact (4,000 m) and footer (4,500 m), both themes.
- **Fix:** on the home page only, keep the packet frame and drop the footer's intro lines, or trim the Contact section to the form and one email line and let the packet carry the links. Other pages keep the footer as it is, since they have no contact section.
- **Effort:** S
- **Helps:** everyone; it shortens the page and makes the ending land.

### 5. /work/ opens with the least telling card
- **Problem:** the first card on /work/ is the ONR research tile: limited detail, abstract art, no metrics and no link to anything I can show. The photos, demos and numbers start further down.
- **Evidence:** /work/ at 1440px, first row: ONR, Because of ADAM, Landspace. RAMpage (first place, demo) and V2V (demo) are in rows 3 and 4.
- **Fix:** order /work/ by strength of evidence: RAMpage, V2V, Because of ADAM, Landspace, BELLHOP, then the rest, with ONR after the work it builds on. Filters stay the same.
- **Effort:** S
- **Helps:** recruiters.

### 6. A recruiter cannot see what role I want or when
- **Problem:** "Open to: internships, research collaborations and freelance web projects" says what, but not when, where or in what field. Recruiters filter on dates and location first.
- **Evidence:** home and /about/, Now strip, item 04. The résumé gives the expected M.S. date (May 2028) but no internship window.
- **Fix:** one line in the Now strip and on the résumé, in my words: the internship window, the fields (for example comms, DSP, embedded), and whether I can relocate or work remotely. Only with facts I confirm (see open questions).
- **Effort:** S
- **Helps:** recruiters.

### 7. /services/ has a layout hole and no price anchor
- **Problem:** "Live and in use" shows four site cards in a three-column grid, so Nicholas Domke sits alone on the second row. And "What does it cost?" answers without any number, which makes small businesses hesitate to write at all.
- **Evidence:** /services/ at 1440px, Recent work section; FAQ, last question.
- **Fix:** a two-by-two or four-across grid for the four sites. For cost, a typical starting range if I am willing to publish one; otherwise say what the quote depends on (pages, forms, editor) and how fast it arrives.
- **Effort:** S
- **Helps:** website clients.

### 8. The V2V case study has no results row
- **Problem:** the V2V case study is one of the strongest pages on the site, with a live demo, but unlike Grace Mae it has no metrics row at the top. Its numbers are real and already in the body: 10 MHz, 64 subcarriers with a 16-sample cyclic prefix, 48 data and 4 pilot subcarriers, run at 2.9 GHz on two ADALM-PLUTO radios, and a 4 second time to impact in the built-in scenario.
- **Evidence:** /work/v2v-bsm-ofdm/ header and Approach section; /work/grace-mae-alterations/ has a three-number metrics row.
- **Fix:** add three or four `metrics` to `v2v-bsm-ofdm.md` from those existing facts. No new numbers.
- **Effort:** S
- **Helps:** recruiters and labs.

## Inspiration: 6 examples

All six load today (checked October 28, 2026). The searches for engineer portfolios returned mostly generic advice pages, so these come from sites I know; the descriptions are my own reading of them, not claims from the search results.

1. **Bartosz Ciechanowski**, https://ciechanow.ski/
   - Does well: long articles where every figure is interactive and sits right next to the sentence it explains.
   - Idea for the ocean concept: turn the sound channel into a short scroll-through explainer, "Why sound carries along 1,000 m", stepping from the profile to Snell's law to a ray that bends back. Textbook physics only, and it could live on the BELLHOP case study.
2. **Jack Schaedler, Seeing Circles, Sines and Signals**, https://jackschaedler.github.io/circles-sines-signals/
   - Does well: builds DSP intuition one small figure at a time, from a rotating phasor up to the DFT.
   - Idea: give the How it works walkthrough its own page, "How my name becomes OFDM", where each of the five steps gets a full-width figure and a sentence or two more, linked from the hero tab.
3. **Josh W. Comeau**, https://www.joshwcomeau.com/
   - Does well: technical writing built around small widgets readers can poke at, with a lot of care for polish.
   - Idea: the short write-up per demo that PLAN.md already lists under Later. One post per demo (spectrum, weapon logic, OFDM), each telling how the firmware or MATLAB was ported to the browser.
4. **Brittany Chiang**, https://brittanychiang.com/
   - Does well: a portfolio that a recruiter can scan in seconds: experience with dates and tools, then projects, all on one fast page.
   - Idea: a compact "at a glance" strip on the home page for the hire audience: current role, degree and date, and the three skills that matter most, linking to the full experience on About. It fits as the 50 m Sunlight zone band beside the Now strip.
5. **Red Blob Games**, https://www.redblobgames.com/
   - Does well: explanations where the reader drags points and the diagram answers at once.
   - Idea: in the sound channel figure, let readers drag a receiver as well as the source and see how many rays arrive and how spread out in time they are. Still a textbook profile, and it shows multipath, the thing my research world cares about, without touching the sponsored work.
6. **Distill**, https://distill.pub/
   - Does well: research writing with clear figures, authors and affiliations, and a citation block at the end.
   - Idea: give the research section and the BELLHOP case study a small citation block: the public ONR announcement, the BELLHOP model itself, and later any thesis or paper. Labs read citations first.

## What's working and should not change

- **The first screen.** At 390px the name, role line, See my work and Résumé are all visible without scrolling, in both themes.
- **The spectrogram is real.** The waterfall is an FFT of the signal that plays, and the How it works tab shows the exact code that runs. This is the most memorable thing on the site and it is honest.
- **Confidentiality handled in the open.** The Limited detail chip, the public-announcement note and the abstract art make the rule visible instead of hiding the work.
- **Case studies with demos.** V2V's receiver demo sits right under the header and is clear and instructive.
- **Client work with numbers.** Commits, pages, hosting cost and fields the owner edits are concrete and checkable.
- **The voice.** Field notes and About read like a person: "Day job plot twist: Land agent", "Test the scary part".
- **Quality floor.** axe is clean on every page in both themes, there is no horizontal scroll at 390px, and mobile Lighthouse sits in the high 90s locally.
- **The descent concept.** Bands, depth labels, the gauge and the packet footer all agree with each other and give the site one idea.
