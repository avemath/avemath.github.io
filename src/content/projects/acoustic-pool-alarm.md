## Why

My little brother Adam drowned in our backyard pool when he was 14 months old. He survived, and his story is part of why my family started [Because of ADAM](/work/because-of-adam/). Drowning is the leading cause of death for children ages 1 to 4 ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/data-research/facts/index.html)), and the moment I want to catch isn't a supervised swim. It's a young child getting into the pool when nobody sees it happen.

## The gap

Every option a family has today fails somewhere. Floating alarms that sense waves go off for wind and rain, so people turn them off. Cameras need light and clear water, and they put a camera on your backyard. Wearables only work if the child is wearing one.

## Why sound

Sound travels well in water. It works at night and in cloudy water, it needs no camera, and it can reach under a pool cover. That's why I want an alarm that listens to the water instead of watching it.

## Where it stands

I haven't built anything yet. Right now I'm reviewing prior work and ASTM F2208, the standard for residential pool alarms, and planning my first bench tests.

## What I'll measure first

These are goals, not results:

- How fast it detects a child entering the water.
- How many false alarms it raises per day of normal backyard noise.
